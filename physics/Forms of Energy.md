# Forms of Energy

Energy is the capacity of a system to perform work. SI unit: joule $[\text{J}]$.

## Kinetic Energy

Energy due to motion:

$$E_K = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$$

where:
- $m$ — mass $[\text{kg}]$
- $v$ — velocity $[\text{m/s}]$

## Potential Energy

### Gravitational Potential Energy

Energy due to position in a gravitational field:

$$E_p = m\,g\,h$$

where:
- $m$ — mass $[\text{kg}]$
- $g$ — gravitational acceleration $[\text{m/s}^2]$
- $h$ — height above reference level $[\text{m}]$

### Elastic Potential Energy

Energy stored in a deformed elastic object (spring, bow):

$$E_{pe} = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$$

where:
- $k$ — spring constant $[\text{N/m}]$
- $x$ — displacement from equilibrium $[\text{m}]$

## Mechanical Energy

Total energy related to motion and position:

$$E_m = E_K + E_p$$

> **Conservation of energy:** In a closed system, total energy remains constant — energy is neither created nor destroyed, only transformed.

## Internal Energy of an Ideal Gas

The **internal energy** $U$ is the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of all particles in a system.

For an **ideal monatomic gas** (no intermolecular potential energy):

$$U = \frac{3}{2}\,n\,R\,T$$

where:
- $n$ — number of moles $[\text{mol}]$
- $R$ — universal gas constant ($8.31\ \text{J/(mol·K)}$)
- $T$ — absolute temperature $[\text{K}]$

Since $U$ depends only on $T$ for an ideal gas, a change in internal energy is:

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2}\,n\,R\,\Delta T$$
