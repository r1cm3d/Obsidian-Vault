# Statics

**Statics** is the study of conditions under which bodies remain in equilibrium — either at rest or in uniform motion.

## Equilibrium of a Particle

A particle is in equilibrium when the **net force** acting on it is zero:

$$\sum \vec{F} = \vec{0}$$

Two types:
- **Static equilibrium**: the particle is at rest ($v = 0$).
- **Dynamic equilibrium**: the particle moves with constant velocity (uniform rectilinear motion, $\vec{v} = \text{const} \neq 0$).

In component form:

$$\sum F_x = 0 \qquad \sum F_y = 0$$

## Torque (Moment of a Force)

The **torque** (or moment) of a force $\vec{F}$ about a pivot point $O$ is:

$$\vec{M}_{F,O} = \vec{F} \cdot d$$

where:
- $\vec{M}_{F,O}$ — torque of force $\vec{F}$ about point $O$ $[\text{N·m}]$
- $\vec{F}$ — applied force $[\text{N}]$
- $d$ — **moment arm** — perpendicular distance from the pivot $O$ to the line of action of $\vec{F}$ $[\text{m}]$

### Sign convention

| Rotation direction | Torque sign |
|---|---|
| Counter-clockwise | Positive ($+$) |
| Clockwise | Negative ($-$) |

If the line of action of $\vec{F}$ passes through $O$, then $d = 0$ and $M = 0$ (no rotation).

### Net torque

For multiple forces acting on a body, the **net torque** about a point $O$ is the algebraic sum:

$$\sum M_O = M_1 + M_2 + M_3 + \cdots$$

## Equilibrium of an Extended (Rigid) Body

Two conditions must be satisfied simultaneously:

### 1st Condition — Translational Equilibrium

$$\sum \vec{F} = \vec{0} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sum F_x = 0 \;\text{ and }\; \sum F_y = 0$$

This prevents translational motion.

### 2nd Condition — Rotational Equilibrium

$$\sum M_O = 0$$

The algebraic sum of all torques about **any** point is zero. This prevents rotational motion.

> Both conditions must hold for full static equilibrium of a rigid body.
