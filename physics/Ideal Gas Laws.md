# Ideal Gas Laws

An **ideal gas** is a theoretical model where gas particles have negligible volume, no intermolecular forces, and undergo perfectly elastic collisions.

The state of a gas is described by three **state variables**: pressure $P$, volume $V$, and temperature $T$.

> **STP (Standard Temperature and Pressure):** $P = 1\ \text{atm}$, $T = 273\ \text{K}$

## Boyle–Mariotte Law (Isothermal Process)

At **constant temperature**, pressure is inversely proportional to volume:

$$P \cdot V = \text{const} \qquad (T = \text{const})$$

$$P_1 \cdot V_1 = P_2 \cdot V_2$$

where:
- $P_1, V_1$ — initial pressure and volume
- $P_2, V_2$ — final pressure and volume

## Charles's Law / 1st Gay-Lussac Law (Isobaric Process)

At **constant pressure**, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature:

$$\frac{V}{T} = \text{const} \qquad (P = \text{const})$$

$$\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$$

where:
- $T$ must be in **Kelvin**

## 2nd Gay-Lussac Law (Isochoric / Isovolumetric Process)

At **constant volume**, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature:

$$\frac{P}{T} = \text{const} \qquad (V = \text{const})$$

$$\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$$

## General Gas Equation

When **all three** state variables change (for a fixed amount of gas):

$$\frac{P_1 \cdot V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 \cdot V_2}{T_2}$$

## Ideal Gas Law (Clapeyron Equation)

When the **amount of gas** (number of moles) may also vary:

$$P \cdot V = n \cdot R \cdot T$$

where:
- $P$ — pressure $[\text{Pa}]$ or $[\text{atm}]$
- $V$ — volume $[\text{m}^3]$ or $[\text{L}]$
- $n$ — number of moles $[\text{mol}]$
- $R$ — universal gas constant
- $T$ — absolute temperature $[\text{K}]$

### Values of $R$

| Units | Value |
|---|---|
| SI ($\text{Pa·m}^3/\text{mol·K}$ or $\text{J/(mol·K)}$) | $R = 8.31$ |
| atm·L/(mol·K) | $R = 0.082$ |

## Summary Table

| Process | Constant | Law | Key relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isothermal | $T$ | Boyle–Mariotte | $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$ |
| Isobaric | $P$ | Charles / 1st Gay-Lussac | $V_1/T_1 = V_2/T_2$ |
| Isochoric | $V$ | 2nd Gay-Lussac | $P_1/T_1 = P_2/T_2$ |
