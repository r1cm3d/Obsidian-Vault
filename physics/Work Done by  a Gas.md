# Work Done by a Gas

## Work at Constant Pressure

When a gas expands or is compressed inside a cylinder with a piston:

$$W = P \cdot \Delta V = P \cdot (V_2 - V_1)$$

where:
- $W$ — work done by the gas $[\text{J}]$
- $P$ — pressure (constant) $[\text{Pa}]$
- $\Delta V = V_2 - V_1$ — change in volume $[\text{m}^3]$

### Derivation from force and displacement

$$W = F \cdot h = (P \cdot A) \cdot h = P \cdot (A \cdot h) = P \cdot \Delta V$$

where:
- $F$ — force exerted by the gas on the piston $[\text{N}]$
- $A$ — cross-sectional area of the piston $[\text{m}^2]$
- $h$ — displacement of the piston $[\text{m}]$

## Sign Convention

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| $\Delta V > 0$ (expansion) | $W > 0$ — gas does work **on** the surroundings |
| $\Delta V < 0$ (compression) | $W < 0$ — surroundings do work **on** the gas |
| $\Delta V = 0$ (isochoric) | $W = 0$ — no work exchanged |

## Graphical Interpretation

In a **P–V diagram**, the work done by the gas equals the **area under the curve** between $V_1$ and $V_2$.

For a general process (non-constant pressure):

$$W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\, dV$$

For a linear process on the P–V diagram, the area forms a trapezoid:

$$W = \frac{(P_1 + P_2)}{2} \cdot (V_2 - V_1)$$
